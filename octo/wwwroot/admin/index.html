Streams & hearts

Playing songs you don't own

What plays when someone picks a song that is not in your library yet.

When a song is missing
YouTube starts at once. Soulseek waits for a lossless copy, then plays.
YouTube shim URL
The yt-dlp helper that finds and streams the song. The default works inside Docker's network.
Soulseek connection
The lossless copy comes through slskd, which is set up on its own page.
Lossless wait seconds
How long to wait before playing the YouTube preview instead. 0 waits as long as it takes.
The lossless download carries on either way.
Keep what is played
Downloads each song played from outside your library, radio included, from the first source in Heart download priority that Octo fetches itself.
Hearts always go first. One played song waits its turn at most; a song played while another waits is skipped and tried again the next time it plays.
Send played albums to Lidarr
Hands the album of each song played from outside your library to Lidarr, once per song.
Every hand-off makes Lidarr search all its indexers, so radio pulls in an album per song.
Record who asked
Shows who hearted a song, in Fetched songs and in the download notification.
Useful on a shared library. Turned off, names stay out of both, and entries already written keep theirs. A download in progress still remembers who asked, in memory only, so the app can show that person its progress.
Favorite downloads when they land
Off by default: a heart on a song you do not have only downloads it, and you heart it again once it is in your library. On, Octo also favorites it in Navidrome when it lands, for the person who hearted it.
A heart on a song you already have is always a favorite, whatever this says, and downloads nothing. Octo's own apps are left out, because their heart means Add. The person's sign-in is held in memory until the song arrives, and a restart drops it.
Skip songs you already have
A heart, an album or a play never downloads a second copy of a song in your library. On by default.
An MP3 or other lossy copy is kept and queued for a higher quality one, when Soulseek or Lidarr can look for it and Better quality is on for you. This holds for hearts through Lidarr too: the album's songs you already have are taken back out of what Lidarr brought. The same song means the same artist and title in the same version, and a length within 8 seconds or the same album.

Powers start-from-a-song queues, dynamic and pinned stations, and search-result discovery. Get a free key →

Discovery is off. Octo needs a free Last.fm API key to find and recommend music. Without it, search and radio only return what's already in your library. The steps are below.

Radio basics

Song radio is a one-time queue requested by a client. Dynamic and pinned radio are reusable stations.

Enable Last.fm radio
Master switch for start-from-a-song queues and all dynamic or pinned stations.
Tracks per station or queue
Maximum tracks in start-from-a-song queues and in dynamic or pinned station snapshots.
Last.fm lookup cache (hours)
How long Octo reuses recommendation responses across radio features.
Wait for a cold station's first track (seconds)
A station with nothing cached yet needs a fetch and a transcode before it can play. The station list answers within this bound and the station appears on the next refresh. 0 waits however long it takes. Default: 8.
Radio loudness target (LUFS)
Every radio track is brought to this EBU R128 loudness before it joins the stream, so local files and previews play at one level. -16 suits headphones and speakers; -14 is what most streaming services use. 0 turns it off.
Refresh stations every (hours)
Rebuilds dynamic and pinned station queues in the background. The current snapshot stays playable until its replacement is ready. Default: 12 hours.

How stations play

Applies to dynamic and pinned stations only. Enable either playback method or both; song radio remains a client-created track queue.

Playlist method
Shows each station in the client's Playlists section. This provides full track controls and preserves normal local-song quality; heart a discovery track to fetch a permanent lossless copy.
Streaming method
Shows each station in the client's Radio section as one continuous MP3 stream. Tracks are transcoded to the quality selected below.
Radio streams cannot add songs to your library. A client sees one continuous stream rather than individual songs, so liking or hearting the current track cannot trigger an Octo download. Open the station in Playlists when you want to heart a song and add it.
Internet radio quality
MP3 bitrate for new continuous streams. Reconnect to apply a change. This does not affect playlist playback or downloaded quality.
Now-playing metadata
Embeds Octo's existing station artist and title in the MP3 stream for radio clients that request ICY metadata. Reconnect after changing this setting.

Tags & genres

How Octo tags the songs it downloads, finds their lyrics, and tidies their genres.

Tags on new downloads

What Octo writes into each song it saves.

Tag language
A two-letter language for genres and other words from Deezer and Last.fm, like en, de or fr.
Without one, the providers pick a language from the server's address; leave it empty to let them. New lookups use it right away. Answers cached in the last 12 hours keep their old language until they expire, and cover art picks up a new language after a restart.
File singles under their title
A song with no album anywhere is filed as a single under its own title.
Only when its source, Deezer and its own tags all name no album, instead of joining the one “[Unknown Album]” Navidrome gives every song without one. Never for a compilation.

Release matching

How Octo decides which release a download is, before it writes a tag.

File a song under its original album
A song that arrived without an album goes under the first release of its recording, even when the file was ripped from a compilation or a later pressing.
Off keeps the album the file’s own tags name when it has one. Either way, a song whose request named an album keeps that album.
Show the original release year
The year is the recording’s first release, so a 2011 remaster of a 1991 album reads 1991.
The pressing’s own date stays in the download’s tag report. Off writes the pressing’s date, and M4A tracks of one album matched to different pressings can then show as two albums.
Preferred countries optional
Two-letter codes, in order, whose pressings win a tie, for example US, XW, GB. Empty means no preference.
Ask MusicBrainz for release details
One request per download, about a second, for the label, catalogue number, barcode, status and track ids of the release that won, and a search by name when the fingerprint named nothing.
Needs no key. Off tags from the fingerprint and Deezer alone.
Measure loudness (ReplayGain)
Each download is measured once and gets ReplayGain track tags, which the Octo apps and most players use to even out volume.
Decodes the whole file beside the lookups, so it rarely adds time. An album fetched whole gets album gain too.
Loudness time limit
Seconds the measurement may take before the download goes on without it. A FLAC on local disk takes a few; a slow mount can take far longer.
Trying it out
Rehearse the matching
Work out what every download would be tagged as and show it under Fetched songs, but write only what Octo wrote before.
ReplayGain, the ISRC and the fingerprint id are still written: none of them depends on the match.
Try it on a song
See how a song would be matched and tagged, without touching anything. A path inside the music folder is fingerprinted and measured; an artist and title alone are matched by name.

Soulseek

Downloads lossless copies through slskd when you heart a song, and shares your music back with the people you download from.

Open slskd

Sharing

Soulseek works because people share. slskd shares your music folder, read-only, so the people you download from can download from you too.

The people you download from can download from you too, read-only, a few songs at a time. Takes effect in seconds.
Off shares nothing at all: slskd forgets your folders and uploads still waiting or under way are cancelled. Octo sets this in slskd's own settings file, slskd.yml, in a part marked as Octo's. Shares you set in slskd.yml yourself are left alone, and this switch stands aside.

Asking slskd…

Test the listening port
Asks Soulseek's own port test whether other people can connect to slskd. On by default.
Octo sends the port number to tools.slsknet.org and nothing else, at most every 6 hours unless you press Test the port. The test checks this server's internet address, so it is only right when slskd reaches the internet the same way as Octo, not through a VPN of its own.

Searching

How long to look, and which files count.

Search wait / per-peer timeout seconds
How long to wait for peers to answer, and how long a download may send nothing before the next of up to 5 peers is tried. A slow download that keeps going is waited for.
Wait for Soulseek hours
When slskd is up but not logged in to Soulseek, hearts wait this long for it before using the next source. 0 means don't wait.
Soulseek's own server goes down for maintenance now and then, for two to four hours. Without a wait, everything hearted in that time lands as a YouTube MP3. Waiting hearts are kept across a restart.
Downloads at once
How many songs may download side by side, 1 to 6. Each lands in its own folder, so they never mix up.
Octo keeps to one at a time until slskd has put a download in its own folder, and goes back to one if slskd ever does not. Moving files into your library stays one at a time either way. 1 is how Octo always worked.
Albums from one folder
A hearted album is searched once, and one person's folder of it is taken in one go. On by default.
Songs that folder lacks, or whose file fails a check, are searched one by one as before. Off searches every song on its own.
Smallest file bytes / file type
Skips clip-sized fakes, and prefers one kind of file.

Verify downloads

Checks a finished download is the song you asked for before it joins your library.

Verify downloads
Fingerprints each Soulseek and YouTube download and asks AcoustID what it really is. Off by default.

Catches covers, karaoke versions, live takes and mislabelled compilation tracks, which all pass the length check. A peer that sends a different recording has that file remembered and never tried again.

A song AcoustID has never heard of is always kept. YouTube downloads are identified too but never rejected, since there is no second copy to try.

AcoustID API key
Free from acoustid.org/new-application.
Without a key the fingerprint lookup is skipped; the length check and its memory of rejected files still work.
Minimum match score percent
How sure AcoustID must be before its answer can reject a file. 85 to begin with.
Raising this makes Octo more permissive, not less: weaker matches are ignored rather than acted on.
Tag from MusicBrainz
On a confident match, write the recording's title, artist, album and year over the source's own tags.
Name files from the match
On a confident match, also name the file from the recording, so the path and the tags agree.
Artist folder, title, album and track number. A canonical name is not always the one you file under, which is why this is off. Only files Octo downloads and confirms; nothing already in your library is renamed.
Catch fake lossless files
Spots an MP3 turned into a FLAC by the gap a lossy encoder leaves up high. On by default.
Checks the spectrum of every download that claims to be lossless. A likely fake is passed over for another lossless copy when a peer has one, and kept, noted as transcoded, when none does. Works without verification or an AcoustID key. A recording that is simply quiet up high is never called fake.
Rejected peers
Files a download proved were the wrong song. Each is forgotten after 30 days.
Clear them if a song has stopped downloading and you think this list is why.
Share fingerprints with AcoustID
Send confirmed fingerprints to AcoustID
When you Keep a song AcoustID was unsure of, send its fingerprint so the next person gets a sure answer.
Only the fingerprint, its length, the MusicBrainz recording id and the file format leave Octo; never a file name, a path or a username. Nothing is sent in rehearsal mode, for a song AcoustID named as something else, or when no single MusicBrainz recording fits.
AcoustID user key
Your own key, from acoustid.org/api-key once signed in. The API key above is not enough on its own.

Cover art

Where covers come from, how large they are, and a wall of the soft ones to replace.

Soft covers

Find albums with small or missing covers, and give them the largest cover out there.

Soft under
·
Find your soft covers
A scan only reads your songs: it looks nothing up and changes nothing, so it is quick.

How it works

A cover is taken only from a release with the same artist and album name: Apple's full-size master, the Cover Art Archive for the release your files name, or Deezer. The largest wins, and an album changes only when it is clearly larger than what it has. Only the front cover is replaced; other pictures stay. Every cover replaced is kept, so Undo puts them all back. Saving a song rewrites its whole tag block, so your own backup of the music folder is the full safety net. Lookups pause about 3 seconds per album to stay inside Apple's limit.

Covers on new downloads

Where Octo looks for a cover when it saves a song, and how large it keeps it.

Cover Art Archive
When a fingerprint named the release, also ask the Cover Art Archive for that release's cover.
The right pressing, with no guessing by name. Apple's master of the same album, the catalog, Deezer, iTunes and Last.fm are asked too, and the largest cover wins.
Replace video covers
A cover that is not square is a video still, so Octo looks for a real one.
When nothing better turns up, its centre square is used, which for a YouTube “Topic” upload is the real cover inside the letterbox.
Write cover.jpg
Also save the cover beside the file, full size, where Navidrome looks first.
Only in the Organized layout, and only in a folder the download created. A cover.jpg Octo wrote gives way to a larger one later; one you put there never changes.
Embed full-size covers
Put the cover in each song at the full size it was found, often 3000 px, instead of 1500 px.
Every song carries its own copy, so this adds a few MB to each. cover.jpg is always full size. Soft covers above embeds the same way.

Lyrics

Where lyrics come from, where they are saved, and a list of the songs that could have better ones.

Better lyrics

Find songs with no lyrics, or lyrics that are not timed word by word, and give them better ones.

Show
·
Find songs that need better lyrics
A scan only reads your songs: it looks nothing up and changes nothing.

How it works

A scan reads each song's tags and the files beside it, and lists the ones with no lyrics, plain lyrics, or lyrics timed only by line. Find better lyrics asks the sources above, in their order, about a second and a half apart, and shows what it found; nothing is written yet. Save writes them where “Save found lyrics” says. Octo replaces only lyrics it wrote: a song whose tags or files hold someone else’s gets the better lyrics beside it as a .lrc, which Navidrome serves first, and its own stay as they are. Everything Save writes over is kept, so Undo puts it back. A song you pick is saved whatever “Write lyrics files beside all library songs” says.

Lyrics

A lyrics file beside each download, and lyrics for any song as it plays, in every app.

Fetch lyrics
Looked up in the background after each download, so a slow lyrics service never holds up the next one.
Synced lyrics go in a .lrc, plain ones in a .txt; lyrics timed word by word are still a normal .lrc that any player shows line by line. Navidrome serves the file without a rescan. A song that already has lyrics is never touched, and a song with none still gets lyrics as it plays.
Sources in order
Asked from the top down. A source that is off is never contacted.
Drag a source to reorder, or use the arrow keys on its handle. “The song’s own lyrics” are the ones already in its tags or in a file beside it; they cannot be switched off, only ranked.
Prefer word-timed lyrics
Lyrics timed word by word win over line-timed ones from a source higher up.
Off, the first timed lyrics win, which asks fewer services per song.
Save found lyrics
Beside the song writes a .lrc or .txt next to it, which Navidrome serves at once.
Inside the song writes them into the song’s own tags, which rewrites the audio file (on a cloud mount it uploads again) and shows after a Navidrome scan, which Octo asks for. Some other players show word timing as <00:12.34> in the text. Lyrics Octo did not write are never replaced: when a song’s tags already hold someone else’s, better ones go beside it instead.
Write lyrics files beside all library songs
Lets a library walk started through the API save lyrics for your own rips and purchases too.
Songs you pick under Better lyrics are saved either way. Lyrics Octo did not write are never replaced.

Once you allow Octo, Spotify sends your browser to an address that will not open. That is expected. Copy the whole address from the address bar and paste it here.
Set up your Spotify app
  1. Signed in to Spotify, open the developer dashboard and choose Create app. Spotify asks that the account that owns the app has Premium.
  2. Any name will do, such as Octo. Tick Web API.
  3. Under Redirect URIs, add exactly http://127.0.0.1/callback and save.
  4. Under User Management, add the email of every Spotify account that will connect, up to five. Your own account is there already.
  5. Copy the Client ID into the field below and save. There is no secret to copy.
Client ID
From your Spotify app's settings. Not a secret: Octo signs in with PKCE, which needs none.
Redirect URI
Exactly as it is on your Spotify app. Spotify takes https, or http on 127.0.0.1, never localhost.
The default, with no port, also lets the Octo apps sign in. If Octo has an https address of its own, use that address followed by /imports/spotify/callback, and Octo finishes the sign-in by itself with nothing to paste.

How it works

Each song goes through Better quality, exactly as the action playlist does it: a lossless copy is looked for, checked to be the same recording and really lossless, and put in the original's place, so the song keeps its plays, favorites and playlist places. The original waits in quarantine until its replacement passes. A song with no lossless copy on any of them stays as it is and is not looked for again by the weekly upgrade for 4 weeks. Copies come from the sources named above, Soulseek first and then Lidarr; while every one of them is out, songs wait.

Library actions

Fix a wrong download from the music app you already use: add the song to one of Octo's action playlists.

This removes files. A removed file moves to a quarantine folder inside your music folder, and is only deleted once it is older than the retention window. It acts on files Octo did not download too, from one tap in a music app with no confirmation. Keep rehearsal mode on until you have read a run.

Quarantine and pacing

Where removed files wait, and how quickly actions run.

Quarantine folder
Inside your music folder. The leading dot keeps it out of Navidrome's scan.
Keep removed files for days
The only step that really deletes. 0 keeps them forever.
Keep the original after a replacement
Off, the retention window is the only safety net for a replaced file.
Check every seconds / at most per check
How quickly an action is noticed, and how many run at once.
The cap stops an app that adds thousands of songs at once from running away with your library.
Upgrade songs a week
Octo looks for a lossless copy of this many MP3 and other lossy songs a week, one at a time, spread across the week, and only while nothing else is downloading. It uses Better quality, so that action must be on, and a song is only replaced when the new file really is better. 0 is off.
Where to look for a higher quality copy
Automatic asks Soulseek first, then Lidarr for what Soulseek cannot find, using whichever of the two is set up.
Lidarr fetches whole albums, so Octo borrows the album: it copies out the one song, deletes the other files that search brought in, and puts the album's monitoring back as it was. A song Lidarr already manages is left to Lidarr. While Soulseek is offline, Lidarr is asked alone.

Lidarr

Hearts through your own Lidarr server, which needs working indexers and a download client.

Lidarr works in whole albums. It cannot look for one song, so a song heart imports the album it is on. Octo never deletes or retags Lidarr's files, but it moves each imported song into your Library folder layout, so Lidarr may later list it as missing.

Raw config

The settings file itself, for editing by hand or copying between servers.

Passwords and keys show here in plain text, except the Navidrome admin password. Don't screenshot or share this page.

Where the file lives

/app/config/settings.json inside the container, and ./octo-config/settings.json beside your docker-compose.yml on the host, where any editor works too. Changes apply within a second; settings marked Restart need a restart.

settings.json

Leave "(saved, not shown)" as it is to keep what is stored: the admin password, the Last.fm shared secret and each Last.fm session key. Anything set here wins over the matching environment variable until it is removed.

Top-level keys: Subsonic, Library, Server, Soulseek, Lidarr, YouTube, LastFm, Metadata, Genre, LibraryActions, Notifications, ListenBrainz. Radio lives in LastFm; pinned stations are the ordered LastFm.DiscoveryStations array, each a whole object with a stable Id, Name, Enabled and Tags.

About

What Octo is, which version is running, and where its settings live.

Octo sits in front of your Navidrome library and adds discovery and downloads to it. Search shows songs you don't own beside your own. Radio on any song finds more through Last.fm and plays previews from YouTube. Heart a song you don't own and Octo fetches it through Soulseek, YouTube or your own Lidarr.
